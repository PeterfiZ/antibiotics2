#1

* Amikacin AMK

Aminoglycosides
Common Brand Names: Amikacin Kabi, Likacin
FDA Cat. D 🤰 Pregnancy Status: Megfontolás / Caution 🍼 Lactation Status: Megfontolás / Caution

Terhesség / Pregnancy: FDA D. Contraindicated in pregnancy due to risk of irreversible fetal ototoxicity and nephrotoxicity.

Szoptatás / Lactation: Use with caution during lactation.

🔬 Mechanism of Action

Concentration-dependent. Semisynthetic derivative of kanamycin. Ineffective in anaerobic environments. Inhibits protein synthesis in Enterococci, but cannot cross the cell wall, so only effective in combination with cell wall synthesis inhibitors.

⚡ Pharmacokinetics & Dynamics

Effect Typebactericidal
Half-life (T1/2)2 - 3 hours
Post-Antibiotic Effect (PAE)Pronounced (3-5 hours)

💊 Dosage & Administration

Adult DosingOnce daily 15 mg/kg i.v. (or divided into 2 doses)
Pediatric Dosing15-20 mg/kg/day i.v. once daily
Renal Dose AdjustmentStrict TDM and dose adjustment required

🎯 Antimicrobial Spectrum

Gram-pozitív: Resistant / Ineffective (-)
Gram-negatív: First-line / Susceptible (+++)
Anaerob: Resistant / Ineffective (-)
Pseudomonas: First-line / Susceptible (+++)
Atípusos: Resistant / Ineffective (-)

Also active against several atypical mycobacteria (e.g., Mycobacterium avium complex) and Mycobacterium tuberculosis.

🩺 Main Clinical Indications

  • Severe, multidrug-resistant Gram-negative nosocomial infections (sepsis, pneumonia, UTI)
  • Combination therapy for multidrug-resistant Pseudomonas aeruginosa infections
  • Combination therapy for MDR-TB (multidrug-resistant tuberculosis)

⚠️ Adverse Effects & Toxicity

  • Nephrotoxicity (kidney damage)
  • Ototoxicity (mainly cochlear: hearing loss)
  • Neuromuscular blockade

💡 Clinical & Didactic Pearls

Most resistant to bacterial resistance mechanisms among aminoglycosides. Should be used only in combination with other effective agents, except in UTI, where it can be used in monotherapy.

#2

* Amoxicillin AMX

Aminopenicillins (broad-spectrum penicillins)
Common Brand Names: Duomox, Amoxicillin-B, Moxilen
FDA Cat. B 🤰 Pregnancy Status: Megengedett / Safe 🍼 Lactation Status: Kompatibilis / Compatible

Terhesség / Pregnancy: FDA B. Widely used and safe throughout pregnancy for respiratory and urinary infections.

Szoptatás / Lactation: Compatible with breastfeeding. Mild loose stools occasionally reported in infant, but nursing can continue.

🔬 Mechanism of Action

Bactericidal. Prevents bacterial cell wall synthesis through inhibition of PBPs. Broader spectrum than Penicillin G, as it penetrates better through the porin channels in the outer membrane of Gram-negative bacteria.

⚡ Pharmacokinetics & Dynamics

Effect Typebactericidal
Half-life (T1/2)1-1.5 hours
Post-Antibiotic Effect (PAE)1.5-2 hours for Gram-positives, minimal for Gram-negatives

💊 Dosage & Administration

Adult Dosing3x 500mg - 1000mg p.o. daily, in severe cases 3x 1000mg
Pediatric Dosing40-90 mg/kg/day p.o., divided into 2-3 doses
Renal Dose AdjustmentGFR < 30 ml/min: dose reduction (e.g., max 2x 500mg daily)

🎯 Antimicrobial Spectrum

Gram-pozitív: First-line / Susceptible (+++)
Gram-negatív: Resistant / Ineffective (-)
Anaerob: Resistant / Ineffective (-)
Pseudomonas: Resistant / Ineffective (-)
Atípusos: Resistant / Ineffective (-)

Drug of choice against Listeria monocytogenes. Unstable against beta-lactamase enzymes!

🩺 Main Clinical Indications

  • Mild forms of community-acquired pneumonia (CAP)
  • Acute otitis media (Middle ear infection)
  • Acute sinusitis
  • Listeria monocytogenes infections (combined with Gentamicin in case of meningitis)

⚠️ Adverse Effects & Toxicity

  • Gastrointestinal complaints (diarrhea, nausea)
  • Maculopapular rash (common, not necessarily allergic, e.g., almost always develops in case of EBV infection/mononucleosis!)
  • Allergic reactions (urticaria, anaphylaxis)

💡 Clinical & Didactic Pearls

Excellent oral absorption, can be taken with or without food. Its parenteral form is mainly available in combination in Hungary.

#3

* Amoxicillin / clavulanic acid (Co-amoxiclav) AMC

Penicillins combined with beta-lactamase inhibitors
Common Brand Names: Aktil, Augmentin, Curam
FDA Cat. B 🤰 Pregnancy Status: Megengedett / Safe 🍼 Lactation Status: Kompatibilis / Compatible

Terhesség / Pregnancy: FDA B. Permitted and safe in pregnancy (use with caution in preterm rupture of membranes).

Szoptatás / Lactation: Compatible with breastfeeding. Insignificant levels in breast milk.

🔬 Mechanism of Action

Bactericidal. Amoxicillin inhibits cell wall synthesis. Clavulanic acid is a 'suicide' beta-lactamase inhibitor that irreversibly binds to bacterial beta-lactamase enzymes (Ambler Class A, including penicillinases, ESBLs to some extent, but not carbapenemases), protecting amoxicillin from degradation.

⚡ Pharmacokinetics & Dynamics

Effect Typebactericidal
Half-life (T1/2)approx. 1 hour (both components)
Post-Antibiotic Effect (PAE)1.5-2 hours for Gram-positives, mild for Gram-negatives

💊 Dosage & Administration

Adult Dosingp.o.: 2x 875/125 mg or 3x 500/125 mg; i.v.: 3x 1.2 g (1000 mg amox + 200 mg clav)
Pediatric Dosing40-90 mg/kg/day p.o. (calculated for amoxicillin), in 2-3 divided doses
Renal Dose AdjustmentGFR < 30 ml/min: Avoid the 875/125 mg formulation, use a lower dose or 500/125 mg every 12-24 hours instead

🎯 Antimicrobial Spectrum

Gram-pozitív: First-line / Susceptible (+++)
Gram-negatív: First-line / Susceptible (+++)
Anaerob: First-line / Susceptible (+++)
Pseudomonas: Resistant / Ineffective (-)
Atípusos: Resistant / Ineffective (-)

One of the most versatile broad-spectrum empirical agents, but ineffective against Pseudomonas, MRSA, and atypical pathogens.

🩺 Main Clinical Indications

  • Community-acquired pneumonia (empirical, in case of comorbidities)
  • Bite wounds (human or animal, due to anaerobic coverage!)
  • Severe acute sinusitis, otitis media
  • Complicated urinary tract infections, pelvic inflammatory diseases
  • Aspiration pneumonia

⚠️ Adverse Effects & Toxicity

  • Diarrhea (very common, due to the effect of clavulanic acid on increasing intestinal motility)
  • Hepatotoxicity (cholestatic hepatitis, especially in older men, with prolonged treatment)
  • Candida superinfection (oral thrush, vaginal yeast infection)

💡 Clinical & Didactic Pearls

The risk of diarrhea can be reduced if the oral formulation is taken immediately at the start of a meal.

* Ampicillin AMP

Aminopenicillins (Broad-spectrum penicillins)
Common Brand Names: Standacillin, Ampicillin Sandoz
FDA Cat. B 🤰 Pregnancy Status: Megengedett / Safe 🍼 Lactation Status: Kompatibilis / Compatible

Terhesség / Pregnancy: FDA B. Safe in pregnancy. First-line therapy for Listeria monocytogenes infections.

Szoptatás / Lactation: Compatible with breastfeeding.

🔬 Mechanism of Action

Bactericidal. Inhibits bacterial cell wall synthesis by binding to penicillin-binding proteins (PBPs), which inhibits transpeptidation.

⚡ Pharmacokinetics & Dynamics

Effect Typebactericidal
Half-life (T1/2)1-1.5 hours
Post-Antibiotic Effect (PAE)1.5-2 hours in Gram-positives, minimal in Gram-negatives

💊 Dosage & Administration

Adult Dosing2-12 g/day i.v., divided into 4-6 doses (12 g/day in severe infections)
Pediatric Dosing100-200 mg/kg/day i.v., divided into 4-6 doses
Renal Dose AdjustmentGFR < 30 ml/min: reduced dose or extended dosing interval (e.g., every 12 hours)

🎯 Antimicrobial Spectrum

Gram-pozitív: First-line / Susceptible (+++)
Gram-negatív: Moderate / Variable (++)
Anaerob: Moderate / Variable (++)
Pseudomonas: Resistant / Ineffective (-)
Atípusos: Resistant / Ineffective (-)

First-line agent for Listeria monocytogenes and Enterococcus faecalis.

🩺 Main Clinical Indications

  • Listeria monocytogenes meningitis (combined with Gentamicin)
  • Enterococcus faecalis endocarditis/sepsis (combined with Ceftriaxone or Gentamicin)
  • Parenteral treatment of mild to moderately severe respiratory and urinary tract infections

⚠️ Adverse Effects & Toxicity

  • Allergic skin rashes (especially in mononucleosis)
  • Gastrointestinal complaints
  • Eosinophilia
  • Interstitial nephritis

💡 Clinical & Didactic Pearls

Poor oral bioavailability compared to amoxicillin, so it is mostly used parenterally (i.v.).

* Ampicillin / sulbactam SAM

Penicillins combined with beta-lactamase inhibitors
Common Brand Names: Unasyn
FDA Cat. B 🤰 Pregnancy Status: Megengedett / Safe 🍼 Lactation Status: Kompatibilis / Compatible

Terhesség / Pregnancy: FDA B. Permitted and safe parenteral beta-lactam combination in pregnancy.

Szoptatás / Lactation: Compatible with breastfeeding.

🔬 Mechanism of Action

Bactericidal. Ampicillin inhibits cell wall synthesis, while sulbactam is a beta-lactamase inhibitor that protects ampicillin from degradation by the most common plasmid-mediated beta-lactamases (Ambler Class A).

⚡ Pharmacokinetics & Dynamics

Effect Typebactericidal
Half-life (T1/2)~1 hour
Post-Antibiotic Effect (PAE)1.5-2 hours in Gram-positives

💊 Dosage & Administration

Adult Dosing3-12 g/day i.v. (ampicillin + sulbactam), divided into 3-4 doses (e.g., 4x 3 g or 3x 1.5-3 g)
Pediatric Dosing150-300 mg/kg/day i.v., divided into 3-4 doses
Renal Dose AdjustmentGFR < 30 ml/min: extend dosing interval to 12-24 hours

🎯 Antimicrobial Spectrum

Gram-pozitív: First-line / Susceptible (+++)
Gram-negatív: First-line / Susceptible (+++)
Anaerob: First-line / Susceptible (+++)
Pseudomonas: Resistant / Ineffective (-)
Atípusos: Resistant / Ineffective (-)

Sulbactam component has intrinsic activity against Acinetobacter baumannii at high doses!

🩺 Main Clinical Indications

  • Intra-abdominal infections
  • Gynecological pelvic inflammatory diseases
  • Acinetobacter baumannii infections (with high-dose sulbactam)
  • Animal bite wounds and aspiration pneumonia

⚠️ Adverse Effects & Toxicity

  • Pain at injection site
  • Diarrhea
  • Elevated liver enzymes
  • Hypersensitivity

💡 Clinical & Didactic Pearls

In Hungary, Unasyn is the most well-known parenteral combination in this group.

#6

* Azithromycin AZM

Macrolides (Azalides)
Common Brand Names: Azibiot, Sumamed, Zitrocin
FDA Cat. B 🤰 Pregnancy Status: Megengedett / Safe 🍼 Lactation Status: Kompatibilis / Compatible

Terhesség / Pregnancy: FDA B. Safe and permitted in pregnancy (first-line for maternal Chlamydia and atypical pneumonia).

Szoptatás / Lactation: Compatible with breastfeeding.

🔬 Mechanism of Action

Bacteriostatic protein synthesis inhibitor (binds to the 50S ribosomal subunit). Long tissue retention: accumulates in lysosomes and white blood cells, which transport it to the site of infection.

⚡ Pharmacokinetics & Dynamics

Effect Typebacteriostatic
Half-life (T1/2)68 hours (extremely long half-life, accumulates in tissues!)
Post-Antibiotic Effect (PAE)Extremely prolonged tissue effect

💊 Dosage & Administration

Adult Dosing500 mg once daily for 3 days (or 500 mg on day 1, then 250 mg on days 2-5); for Chlamydia urethritis, a single dose of 1 g p.o.
Pediatric Dosing10 mg/kg/day p.o. for 3 days (or 10 mg/kg on day 1, then 5 mg/kg on days 2-5)
Renal Dose AdjustmentNo dosage adjustment required in renal impairment (primarily excreted in bile)

🎯 Antimicrobial Spectrum

Gram-pozitív: Resistant / Ineffective (-)
Gram-negatív: First-line / Susceptible (+++)
Anaerob: Resistant / Ineffective (-)
Pseudomonas: Resistant / Ineffective (-)
Atípusos: First-line / Susceptible (+++)

Extremely convenient dosing (a 3-day course corresponds to a 7-10 day standard treatment).

🩺 Main Clinical Indications

  • Atypical community-acquired pneumonia (CAP)
  • Non-gonococcal urethritis / cervicitis caused by Chlamydia trachomatis (1g stat p.o.)
  • First-line treatment and prophylaxis of pertussis (whooping cough)
  • Traveler's diarrhea (in case of suspected Campylobacter)

⚠️ Adverse Effects & Toxicity

  • QT interval prolongation (although considered slightly safer than clarithromycin)
  • Gastrointestinal intolerance (milder than other macrolides)
  • Otitis / hearing impairment (with long-term use)

💡 Clinical & Didactic Pearls

Minimal drug interaction compared to other macrolides, as it does not significantly inhibit the CYP3A4 system.

#7

* Benzylpenicillin (Penicillin G) PEN

Natural penicillins
Common Brand Names: Penicillin G Sandoz
FDA Cat. B 🤰 Pregnancy Status: Megengedett / Safe 🍼 Lactation Status: Kompatibilis / Compatible

Terhesség / Pregnancy: FDA B. Safe and permitted in pregnancy. Gold standard for syphilis and GBS prophylaxis.

Szoptatás / Lactation: Compatible with breastfeeding. Excreted into breast milk in trace amounts.

🔬 Mechanism of Action

Bactericidal. Inhibits bacterial cell wall synthesis: irreversibly binds to penicillin-binding proteins (PBPs), preventing the cross-linking of peptidoglycan chains (transpeptidation), which leads to cell wall lysis.

⚡ Pharmacokinetics & Dynamics

Effect Typebactericidal
Half-life (T1/2)30-45 minutes (prolonged in renal failure)
Post-Antibiotic Effect (PAE)Pronounced in Gram-positives (1.5-3 hours), absent in Gram-negatives

💊 Dosage & Administration

Adult Dosing10-24 million IU (International Units) daily i.v., divided into 4-6 doses
Pediatric Dosing100,000 - 400,000 IU/kg daily i.v., divided into 4-6 doses
Renal Dose AdjustmentGFR < 50 ml/min: reduced dose or prolonged dosing interval (e.g., 50-75% dose)

🎯 Antimicrobial Spectrum

Gram-pozitív: First-line / Susceptible (+++)
Gram-negatív: Resistant / Ineffective (-)
Anaerob: Resistant / Ineffective (-)
Pseudomonas: Resistant / Ineffective (-)
Atípusos: Resistant / Ineffective (-)

First-line agent for Treponema pallidum (syphilis) and Borrelia burgdorferi (Lyme disease). Excellent against Streptococci (groups A, B, C, G).

🩺 Main Clinical Indications

  • Infective endocarditis (susceptible Streptococcus and Enterococcus strains)
  • Meningitis (susceptible strains of Neisseria meningitidis and Streptococcus pneumoniae)
  • Syphilis (Neurosyphilis)
  • Gas gangrene (Clostridium perfringens)

⚠️ Adverse Effects & Toxicity

  • Hypersensitivity reactions (skin rash, anaphylaxis: 0.05-0.2%)
  • Neurotoxicity at high doses (seizures, especially in renal failure)
  • Electrolyte imbalance (hyperkalemia in the case of potassium penicillin)
  • Jarisch-Herxheimer reaction during syphilis treatment

💡 Clinical & Didactic Pearls

For parenteral administration only. Acid-labile, therefore ineffective orally.

* Cefazolin CZ

1st generation Cephalosporins
Common Brand Names: Cefazolin Kabi, Sandoz Cefazolin
FDA Cat. B 🤰 Pregnancy Status: Megengedett / Safe 🍼 Lactation Status: Kompatibilis / Compatible

Terhesség / Pregnancy: FDA B. Safe and permitted in pregnancy; gold standard perioperative prophylaxis in cesarean section.

Szoptatás / Lactation: Compatible with breastfeeding.

🔬 Mechanism of Action

Bactericidal cell wall synthesis inhibitor. Binds strongly to PBP-1 and PBP-3 proteins. Stable against penicillinases produced by Staphylococcus aureus.

⚡ Pharmacokinetics & Dynamics

Effect Typebactericidal
Half-life (T1/2)1.5 - 2 hours (relatively long among cephalosporins)
Post-Antibiotic Effect (PAE)1.5-3 hours for Gram-positives

💊 Dosage & Administration

Adult Dosing1g - 2g i.v. 3 times daily; For surgical prophylaxis: 2g single i.v. bolus (30-60 minutes before surgery)
Pediatric Dosing50-100 mg/kg/day i.v., divided into 3-4 doses
Renal Dose AdjustmentGFR < 30 ml/min: reduced dose and/or prolonged interval

🎯 Antimicrobial Spectrum

Gram-pozitív: First-line / Susceptible (+++)
Gram-negatív: Resistant / Ineffective (-)
Anaerob: Resistant / Ineffective (-)
Pseudomonas: Resistant / Ineffective (-)
Atípusos: Resistant / Ineffective (-)

Ineffective against MRSA, Enterococci, and Listeria! (All cephalosporins are ineffective against Enterococcus and Listeria!)

🩺 Main Clinical Indications

  • Surgical prophylaxis (the gold standard for almost all types of surgery)
  • Systemic infections caused by MSSA (osteomyelitis, arthritis, endocarditis, sepsis)
  • Mild-to-moderate skin and soft tissue infections

⚠️ Adverse Effects & Toxicity

  • Allergy (cross-reactivity with penicillin approx. 1-3%)
  • Pain at the injection site, thrombophlebitis
  • Nephrotoxicity (rare, but may occur at high doses)

💡 Clinical & Didactic Pearls

Does not penetrate the blood-brain barrier; unsuitable for the treatment of meningitis.

#9

* Cefepime FEP

4th generation Cephalosporins
Common Brand Names: Cefepim Kabi, Maxipime
FDA Cat. B 🤰 Pregnancy Status: Megengedett / Safe 🍼 Lactation Status: Kompatibilis / Compatible

Terhesség / Pregnancy: FDA B. Permitted and safe in pregnancy (4th gen anti-pseudomonal cephalosporin).

Szoptatás / Lactation: Compatible with breastfeeding.

🔬 Mechanism of Action

Bactericidal. It has a zwitterionic structure, allowing extremely rapid penetration through the porin channels of Gram-negative bacteria. It is highly stable against chromosomal AmpC beta-lactamases and resistant to many plasmid-mediated beta-lactamases.

⚡ Pharmacokinetics & Dynamics

Effect Typebactericidal
Half-life (T1/2)2 hours
Post-Antibiotic Effect (PAE)Approx. 1-1.5 hours for Gram-negatives

💊 Dosage & Administration

Adult Dosing1g - 2g i.v. 2 or 3 times daily (3x 2g in severe infection/suspected Pseudomonas)
Pediatric Dosing100-150 mg/kg/day i.v., divided into 2-3 doses
Renal Dose AdjustmentGFR < 50 ml/min: strict dose reduction (risk of neurotoxicity!)

🎯 Antimicrobial Spectrum

Gram-pozitív: First-line / Susceptible (+++)
Gram-negatív: First-line / Susceptible (+++)
Anaerob: Resistant / Ineffective (-)
Pseudomonas: First-line / Susceptible (+++)
Atípusos: Resistant / Ineffective (-)

It has a much better Gram-positive activity than ceftazidime.

🩺 Main Clinical Indications

  • Severe nosocomial pneumonia
  • Febrile neutropenia (empirical monotherapy)
  • Complicated intra-abdominal infections (combined with metronidazole)
  • Complicated urinary tract infections, pyelonephritis

⚠️ Adverse Effects & Toxicity

  • Neurotoxicity: non-convulsive status epilepticus, confusion, myoclonus (especially in renal impairment if the dose is not reduced!)
  • Allergic reactions, diarrhea

💡 Clinical & Didactic Pearls

Very safe when dosed appropriately for renal function, but the neurological status of elderly patients with renal impairment should be monitored.

* Cefepime / tazobactam FEP-TAN

4th-generation cephalosporin and beta-lactamase inhibitor combinations
Common Brand Names: Cefepime/Tazobactam (abroad)
FDA Cat. B 🤰 Pregnancy Status: Megengedett / Safe 🍼 Lactation Status: Kompatibilis / Compatible

Terhesség / Pregnancy: FDA B. Permitted in pregnancy.

Szoptatás / Lactation: Compatible with breastfeeding.

🔬 Mechanism of Action

Bactericidal. Fourth-generation cefepime is resistant to AmpC beta-lactamases, while tazobactam inhibits ESBL enzymes, making the combination excellent against multidrug-resistant Gram-negative bacteria.

⚡ Pharmacokinetics & Dynamics

Effect Typebactericidal
Half-life (T1/2)approx. 2 hours
Post-Antibiotic Effect (PAE)Moderate

💊 Dosage & Administration

Adult Dosing2x or 3x 2 g / 0.25 g i.v. daily
Pediatric Dosing150 mg/kg/day i.v. divided into 3 doses
Renal Dose AdjustmentGFR < 50 ml/min: reduced dose and longer dosing interval recommended

🎯 Antimicrobial Spectrum

Gram-pozitív: First-line / Susceptible (+++)
Gram-negatív: First-line / Susceptible (+++)
Anaerob: Moderate / Variable (++)
Pseudomonas: First-line / Susceptible (+++)
Atípusos: Resistant / Ineffective (-)

Specifically developed against ESBL-producing and AmpC-overproducing Enterobacterales strains, as well as Pseudomonas aeruginosa.

🩺 Main Clinical Indications

  • Severe hospital-acquired infections
  • Nosocomial pneumonia
  • Complicated intra-abdominal infections
  • Empiric treatment of febrile neutropenia

⚠️ Adverse Effects & Toxicity

  • Neurotoxicity (seizures, encephalopathy - especially in renal impairment!)
  • Diarrhea
  • Elevated liver function tests

💡 Clinical & Didactic Pearls

A new-generation weapon against Gram-negative resistance.

#11

* Cefiderocol FDC

Siderophore cephalosporins (5th-generation derivative)
Common Brand Names: Fetcroja
FDA Cat. C 🤰 Pregnancy Status: Megfontolás / Caution 🍼 Lactation Status: Megfontolás / Caution

Terhesség / Pregnancy: FDA C. Limited human data; reserve for life-threatening XDR infections without safer alternatives.

Szoptatás / Lactation: Not recommended during breastfeeding due to insufficient data.

🔬 Mechanism of Action

Bactericidal. A unique 'Trojan horse' mechanism: cefiderocol is a siderophore-conjugated cephalosporin that actively binds extracellular bacterial iron. The bacterium's own active iron transport system (tonB-dependent receptors) brings it into the periplasmic space, where it bypasses efflux pumps and porin mutations, then inhibits cell wall synthesis by binding to PBP-3. It is extremely stable against all beta-lactamases (including metallo-beta-lactamases!).

⚡ Pharmacokinetics & Dynamics

Effect Typebactericidal
Half-life (T1/2)2 - 2.5 hours
Post-Antibiotic Effect (PAE)Marked against Gram-negative bacteria

💊 Dosage & Administration

Adult Dosing3x 2 g i.v. daily in a 3-hour extended infusion
Pediatric DosingIts safety and dosing in children are not yet established
Renal Dose AdjustmentStrict dose adjustment is required both in renal impairment and in augmented renal clearance (ARC, GFR > 120 ml/min, in which case 4x 2 g/day!)

🎯 Antimicrobial Spectrum

Gram-pozitív: Resistant / Ineffective (-)
Gram-negatív: First-line / Susceptible (+++)
Anaerob: Resistant / Ineffective (-)
Pseudomonas: First-line / Susceptible (+++)
Atípusos: Resistant / Ineffective (-)

A last-resort weapon exclusively against multidrug-resistant (MDR) and pandrug-resistant (PDR) Gram-negative bacteria.

🩺 Main Clinical Indications

  • Severe aerobic Gram-negative infections in adults when treatment options are limited
  • Hospital-acquired and ventilator-associated pneumonia (HAP/VAP)
  • Complicated urinary tract infections and urosepsis caused by multidrug-resistant Gram-negative pathogens (e.g. NDM-producing Klebsiella or Pseudomonas)

⚠️ Adverse Effects & Toxicity

  • Diarrhea, nausea
  • Positive Coombs test
  • Elevation of liver function tests
  • Hypokalaemia
  • Rare neurotoxicity (seizures - at high doses)
